How Compound Interest Works: Formulas, Rule of 72 & Real Tables

Investing & Savings · 8 min read · Updated November 18, 2026

Simple interest pays returns only on your original deposit. Compound interest pays returns on your original principal plus every dollar of previously earned interest—turning linear savings into an exponential curve over multi-decade horizons.

A = P × (1 + r / n)n × t  |  P = principal, r = annual rate, n = compounding periods/yr, t = years

Why Time Beats Contribution Size: Early vs. Late Start

Consider two investors earning an 8% annualized return compounded monthly:

  • Investor A (Early Starter): Invests $400/month from age 25 to 35 (only 10 years, $48,000 total out of pocket), then stops adding money completely until age 65.
  • Investor B (Late Starter): Waits until age 35, then invests $400/month for 30 straight years until age 65 ($144,000 out of pocket—three times as much cash).

Despite depositing $96,000 less out of pocket, Investor A finishes with over $200,000 more purely because those early dollars had an extra decade to compound.

The Rule of 72 for Instant Mental Math

To check how fast any investment doubles—or how fast inflation cuts your cash purchasing power in half—divide 72 by the annual rate:

  • 4% High-Yield Savings: 72 ÷ 4 = 18.0 years to double
  • 8% Diversified Index Portfolio: 72 ÷ 8 = 9.0 years to double
  • 10% Long-Term Equity Growth: 72 ÷ 10 = 7.2 years to double
  • 24% Credit Card Debt: 72 ÷ 24 = 3.0 years for unpaid balances to double against you

Don't Forget Inflation & Expense Ratios

Always subtract annual fund fees and expected inflation (~3%) to inspect real purchasing-power growth. An 8% nominal return minus 3% inflation gives a ~5% real compound rate. Use the Mutual Fund Calculator and Retirement Calculator to stress-test conservative returns.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Rule of 72?▶
Divide 72 by your annual percentage return to estimate how many years it takes your money to double. At an 8% annual return, money doubles roughly every 72 / 8 = 9 years.
How much difference does daily vs. monthly compounding make?▶
At 8% nominal APR, annual compounding yields 8.00% APY, monthly compounding yields 8.30% APY, and daily compounding yields 8.33% APY. Starting early and investing consistently matter far more than daily vs. monthly intervals.
