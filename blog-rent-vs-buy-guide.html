Rent vs. Buy a Home: The 5% Rule & True Break-Even Horizon

Loans & Mortgages · 8 min read · Updated November 18, 2026

The cliché "renting is throwing money away" ignores one huge reality: homeownership also carries massive unrecoverable costs—mortgage interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance, HOA dues, and closing fees that build zero equity.

The 5% Rule: Apples-to-Apples Monthly Comparison

Portfolio managers often apply the 5% Rule to check whether local home prices are cheap or expensive relative to rents:

  • ~1% annually in unrecoverable property taxes
  • ~1% annually in ongoing repairs, roofs, HVAC, and maintenance
  • ~3% annually in mortgage interest & down-payment opportunity cost

Example on a $450,000 Home:

($450,000 × 5%) ÷ 12 = $1,875 / month in unrecoverable owner costs. If you can rent an equivalent home for $1,650/month and faithfully invest the difference + down payment, renting can come out ahead over short horizons.

Why Holding Period Changes Everything

Selling a home typically costs 6%–8% in agent commissions, transfer taxes, and prep work—on top of 2%–4% buyer closing costs upfront. On a $400,000 house, that round-trip friction exceeds $36,000. Frequently asked questions

What is the 5% Rule for renting vs. buying?▶
Multiply a home's price by 5% and divide by 12 to estimate unrecoverable monthly ownership costs (property tax ~1%, maintenance ~1%, and cost of capital/interest ~3%). If you can rent a similar property for less than that figure, renting may build more wealth if you invest the savings.
How long do I typically need to own a home to break even?▶
Because buyer + seller closing costs and realtor commissions consume 7%–10% of property value round-trip, buying usually takes 5 to 7+ years to outperform renting financially.
