Understanding percentages

A percentage is a dimensionless ratio expressed as a fraction of 100 (from the Latin per centum meaning "by the hundred"). Saying 20% of $85 means taking 20 parts out of 100 equal parts of $85, giving $17.00. Percentages allow standard comparisons across different baselines.

Percentage change explained

Percentage change measures relative growth or reduction compared to the initial baseline: % Change = ((New Value − Initial Value) / Initial Value) × 100.

A positive percentage indicates an increase (growth/markup), whereas a negative percentage represents a decrease (decline/discount). Notice that a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not return to the starting number (e.g. 100 + 50% = 150; 150 − 50% = 75).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?▶
Multiply the number by the percentage divided by 100. For example 20% of 250 = 250 × 20/100 = 50.
How do I calculate percentage change?▶
Use the formula (New - Old) / Old × 100. From 50 to 65 that is (65 - 50)/50 × 100 = +30% increase.
What percent is 25 of 200?▶
Divide 25 by 200 and multiply by 100: (25 / 200) × 100 = 12.5%.
How does reverse percentage work?▶
If an item costs $120 after a 20% discount, the original price was $120 / (1 - 0.20) = $150.
