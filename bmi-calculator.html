How Body Mass Index (BMI) Works

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a rapid screening metric developed to estimate whether an adult's weight falls inside a healthy proportion relative to their height. Because tall people naturally carry more tissue mass than shorter individuals, squaring height in meters normalizes body weight across statures.

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²  |  US: BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ [height (in)]²

While BMI is trusted worldwide by clinicians for population screening, it does not distinguish between dense skeletal muscle and body fat. For a deeper body composition check, combine your BMI with our Body Fat Calculator or read our BMI vs. Body Fat Guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI range for adults?▶
For most adults age 20 and older, the World Health Organization defines a healthy BMI as 18.5 to 24.9. Values below 18.5 are underweight, 25.0 to 29.9 are overweight, and 30.0 or higher fall in the obese range.
What is BMI Prime?▶
BMI Prime is the ratio of your actual BMI to the upper limit of normal BMI (25.0). A BMI Prime below 0.74 indicates underweight, 0.74 to 1.00 is healthy, and values above 1.00 show how far above the normal threshold your weight sits.
Why can athletes have a high BMI?▶
Muscle tissue is roughly 18% denser than fat tissue. Muscular athletes often score in the 25–28 BMI range despite having low body fat. Combining BMI with waist-to-height ratio or the U.S. Navy body fat test gives a clearer picture.
