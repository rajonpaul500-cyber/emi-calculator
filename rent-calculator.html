What is the 30% rent rule?

The 30% rule is a widely used budgeting guideline: keep your housing costs at or below 30% of your gross monthly income. It leaves room for food, transport, savings and unexpected expenses. In expensive cities you may need to go higher, but the smaller the percentage, the more comfortable your budget.

Rent Calculator FAQ

Should I include utilities in my rent budget?▶
Yes. Electricity, water, gas and internet add up quickly. This calculator adds utilities to your rent so you see your true housing cost each month.
Is the 30% rule always accurate?▶
It is a guideline, not a law. High-income earners can often afford a higher percentage, while lower-income households may need to keep it well below 30% to make ends meet.

The classic advice: keep housing under 30% of gross income. This rent calculator turns your income and expenses into a realistic monthly rent figure — including utilities and deposit, plus smart roommate-splitting guidance.

The 30% rule and reality

30% of gross income is the affordability benchmark: $40,000/month income → $12,000 rent.

In high-cost cities, many renters hit 40–50%; that's the reality of metro cities — compensate by cutting other variable costs.

The 3× income rule for annual rent is another check: annual rent should be ≤ 3× annual gross income.

Hidden costs renters forget

Security deposits (usually 1–3 months' rent), brokerage, utilities, internet and moving costs.

Renter's insurance is cheap insurance against theft and liability — typically under $5,000/year.

How to use this calculator

Pro tips

  • Target 25–30% of income for rent; the 20% buffer covers utilities and rent increases.
  • Negotiate renewals: 5% less than asking is often accepted to avoid vacancy.
  • Roommate splitting by room size (40/60 instead of 50/50) is fairer in 2-BHKs with a master bedroom.

Frequently asked questions

How much rent can I afford?▶
Most advisors cap rent at 30% of gross income. On $60,000/month income, that's $18,000. Factor utilities separately — they can add $2,000–5,000.
What is the rule of thumb for rent?▶
Spend no more than 30% of gross income on housing, including rent and utilities. Some metro renters push to 40% by trimming food and travel budgets.
