Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball: Fastest Way to Pay Off Credit Cards

Debt & Budget · 6 min read · Updated November 18, 2026

Paying off multiple credit cards, auto loans, or personal balances comes down to a simple decision: once you pay the minimum required on every account, where should every extra dollar go? Two proven systems dominate personal finance.

Method 1: Debt Avalanche (Minimum Interest Cost)

List debts from highest APR to lowest APR. Direct 100% of extra cash at the highest-interest balance first regardless of balance size. Once it hits zero, roll its entire payment into the next highest APR.

Method 2: Debt Snowball (Fastest Early Wins)

List debts from smallest balance to largest balance, ignoring interest rates. Eliminating the smallest account first frees up cash flow and drops the number of bills you juggle right away.

Ranking OrderHighest APR firstSmallest balance first

Frequently asked questions

Which debt payoff method saves the most money?▶
The Debt Avalanche method always minimizes total interest paid because every extra dollar kills your highest-APR balance first.
When is the Debt Snowball better?▶
If you have several small $300–$1,000 balances draining focus or struggle to stay motivated, knocking out two small cards in 60 days creates momentum that keeps many people consistent to debt freedom.
