The meaning of an exponent

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself. For example 2^3 means 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. The exponent is sometimes called the 'power'.

The calculator also supports negative exponents, where b^-n equals 1 / b^n (for example 2^-2 = 1/4), and fractional exponents, where b^(1/n) is the nth root of b (for example 8^(1/3) = 2).

Useful exponent rules

Multiplying powers with the same base adds the exponents: x^a × x^b = x^(a+b). Dividing subtracts them: x^a ÷ x^b = x^(a-b).

A power of a power multiplies exponents: (x^a)^b = x^(a·b). Any non-zero number to the power of zero equals 1, and x^1 equals x.

Frequently asked questions

What is an exponent?▶
An exponent says how many times to multiply a base by itself. In 2^5, the base 2 is multiplied 5 times: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.
What is a negative exponent?▶
A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the positive power: 2^-3 = 1/(2^3) = 1/8. This is useful for writing very small numbers.
What is a fractional exponent?▶
A fractional exponent represents a root: x^(1/2) is the square root of x, and x^(1/3) is the cube root. More generally x^(m/n) is the nth root of x^m.
What is 2 to the power of 10?▶
2^10 = 1024, the familiar value behind kilobytes in computing (1 KiB = 1024 bytes).
What is any number to the power of zero?▶
Any non-zero number raised to the power of zero equals 1. For example 5^0 = 1 and 1000^0 = 1.
