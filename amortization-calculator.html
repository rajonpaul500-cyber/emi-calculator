How Amortization Works: The Math Behind Your Loan

Amortization is the process of spreading out a loan into a series of equal periodic payments. While each periodic payment is typically identical (on a fixed-rate loan), the internal ratio between principal and interest shifts continuously over the life of the debt.

The Front-Loaded Interest Phenomenon

In the beginning of your loan, your outstanding principal balance is at its absolute highest. Because interest is calculated as a percentage of the remaining balance each period (Interest = Current Balance × Periodic Rate), the majority of your early payments go purely to the bank for finance charges. As the principal drops, less interest accrues each month, allowing a steadily increasing percentage of your payment to build equity.

The Real Power of Extra Payments

Any payment you make above and beyond your scheduled payment is applied 100% directly toward reducing your principal balance. This has a compounding domino effect:

  • Immediate Balance Reduction: Lowers the base on which all future interest is calculated.
  • Permanent Interest Elimination: You never have to pay interest on that paid-off portion for all remaining years of the loan.
  • Shortened Loan Term: Even an extra $100/month on a typical $400,000, 30-year fixed mortgage at 6.5% can pay off your home over 4 years earlier and save more than $60,000 in interest!

Amortization Calculator FAQ

What is the difference between an amortization schedule and a payment schedule?▶
A basic payment schedule only lists due dates and the total amount owed. An amortization schedule goes deeper, breaking down each payment into its exact principal and interest portions, tracking your remaining balance, and displaying cumulative interest paid over the life of the loan.
Can I download or print this amortization schedule?▶
Yes! You can click Download CSV Schedule to export the full table for Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets, or click Print Schedule for a clean, ad-free printable document.
Are mortgage interest payments tax-deductible in the US?▶
In the United States, homeowners who itemize their tax deductions on Schedule A (Form 1040) can typically deduct interest paid on qualified home acquisition debt up to $750,000 (or $375,000 if married filing separately). Consult your CPA or tax professional for advice specific to your financial situation.
What happens if I make bi-weekly mortgage payments?▶
When you switch to bi-weekly payments (paying half of your monthly payment every two weeks), you make 26 half-payments a year. That equals 13 full payments per year instead of 12. That single extra monthly payment per year accelerates equity growth and can shorten a 30-year mortgage by 4 to 6 years!

Amortization Calculator — your complete loan repayment schedule

An amortization schedule is the month-by-month map of your loan: how much of each payment goes to principal, how much to interest, and your remaining balance. This calculator produces the full schedule instantly and shows how extra payments shorten the term and slash total interest.

Reading an amortization schedule

Each row shows payment number, EMI, principal portion, interest portion and outstanding balance.

Early rows are interest-heavy; later rows flip to principal-heavy. Interest is calculated on the declining balance each month.

The total interest is the sum of the interest column — this is what costs more than double on long loans.

The power of extra payments

Add just 5% extra to each payment and a 20-year loan finishes about 3 years early, saving 10–15% of total interest.

A single yearly lump sum (like a tax refund or work bonus) applied to principal has an even larger effect because it avoids future interest on that amount.

Two strategies to compare: reduce monthly payment (recast) vs shorten term. Shortening term always saves more interest.

Pro tips

  • Use the schedule to find your break-even month for refinancing.
  • Make sure extra payments are applied to principal, not 'paid ahead'.
  • For a 30-year fixed mortgage, extra payments made in the first 5 to 7 years save the most interest over time.

Frequently asked questions

What is an amortization schedule used for?▶
It shows the exact repayment path of a loan, helping borrowers see equity growth, plan prepayments and compare loan offers by total interest.
Does the payment change during the loan?▶
For fixed-rate loans the total payment is constant, but the principal-interest split changes each month. For adjustable-rate loans, the payment changes when interest rates reset.
