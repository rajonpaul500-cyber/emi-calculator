Calorie Deficit, BMR & TDEE Explained: Sustainable Fat Loss Math

Health & Fitness · 7 min read · Updated November 18, 2026

Every sustainable fat-loss or muscle-building plan follows energy balance physics: consistently consuming fewer calories than your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) forces your body to draw energy from stored adipose tissue.

Step 1: Compute Your Resting BMR

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the calorie floor required purely to keep your heart, brain, liver, and lungs running over 24 hours. Clinical research confirms the Mifflin-St Jeor equation is the most reliable standard formula:

Men: (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) + 5  |  Women: (10 × kg) + (6.25 × cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Step 2: Multiply by Realistic Activity Factor

Most people overestimate workout burn. Unless you work a physically demanding trade on your feet all day, choosing Lightly Active (1.375) or Moderately Active (1.55) in the BMR Calculator produces far more reliable real-world numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my weight loss stall even on a 500-calorie deficit?▶
As body weight drops, your resting BMR declines slightly and non-exercise movement (NEAT) naturally drops. Recalculate your TDEE every 5–10 lbs lost and double-check cooking oils, sauces, and liquid calories.
How much protein should I eat during a calorie deficit?▶
Research supports 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight (roughly 0.7 to 1.0 g per pound) to protect lean muscle tissue and control hunger while losing fat.
